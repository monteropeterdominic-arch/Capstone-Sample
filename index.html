👋 Welcome!

This map shows where jackfruit trees get sick from a germ called E. carotovora.

  1. 🌳
    Look at the towers. Each tower is a farm with sick trees. Tall and red means very sick.
  2. 👆
    Tap a tower to see that farm's story.
  3. 🔁
    Switch between How bad? and How often? at the top.

How the study works

Two OLS models share eight explanatory variables: maximum temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, rainfall, distance to road, distance to river, distance to irrigation, and elevation.

  • Model 1 (How bad?): percentage of area affected by E. carotovora (severity).
  • Model 2 (How often?): frequency of disease incidence (number of cases).

Data were natural-log (ln) transformed in SPSS, and outliers were removed using box plots.

Supporting methods

  • Kernel density: hotspot map ("worst spots"). Mapping only, not part of the regression.
  • Near tool: distance from each record to the nearest road, river, irrigation line and elevation contour.
  • Moran's I: checks clustering. Distance to road and elevation first showed clustering.
  • R² / Adjusted R²: model fit. VIF: all below 7.5.
  • Joint F: overall significance. Koenker (BP): not significant, so GWR was not needed.

About this sample

Boundaries: PSGC (faeldon/philippines-json-maps). Rivers and irrigation canals: HOTOSM waterways. Roads: OpenStreetMap. Elevation: ALOS AW3D30. Processed in QGIS. Farm points, weather values and OLS numbers are placeholders (OLS values from Idris et al., 2023, Johor).
